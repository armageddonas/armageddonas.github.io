Rolls and results

  • Pool: Attribute + Skill in d6. Every 5 or 6 is a success.
  • Untrained skills: When you roll a skill you have no dots in, take −1d for a Physical or Social skill, or −3d for a Mental skill.
  • Chance die: If penalties reduce your pool to 0 dice or fewer, roll a single d6 instead. Only a 6 is a success. A 1 is a Complication with no success.
  • Rerolls: Each die can be rerolled only once per roll.
  • Exploding dice: When a rule says 6s explode, each 6 adds another die to the roll, which can explode too. Exploding dice are new dice, not rerolls. The stakes die and chance dice never explode.
  • Contested rolls: Both sides roll. The side with more successes wins; the margin is the net successes. Ties go to the side that acts: the attacker, or whoever is trying to do something to the other. A tie is a win with 0 net successes, so an attack that ties is a hit with 0 net successes.
  • Success with Opportunity: Any roll with 5 or more successes is a success with Opportunity. The stakes die can also give an Opportunity (below), even on a failed roll. Talents that "expand the Opportunity range" lower this threshold by 1 per step (to 4+, then 3+). The threshold never goes below 3.
  • Stakes die: When a character raises the stakes, they roll an extra d6 of a different colour along with their pool:
    • 5–6: an Opportunity, but no success.
    • 1–2: a Complication, which also counts as a success.
    • 3–4: nothing.

    Overall, the stakes die succeeds as often as a normal die; what it adds is the swing. An Opportunity from the stakes die applies even if the roll fails: you don't get what you wanted, but you gain something else.

  • Raising the stakes: The GM raises the stakes on a roll when the outcome could change the situation in a big way: a desperate leap, a lie to someone powerful, a blow that could end a fight. Once per day, a player can also choose to raise the stakes on their own roll, before rolling. Talents and effects can add more uses. A roll never has more than one stakes die.
  • Opportunities and Complications: On an Opportunity, the player chooses a benefit that fits the roll (an extra effect, a useful detail, a better position), and the GM approves it. On a Complication, the GM adds a setback that fits the roll (a lost item, an alerted guard, a slip). A Complication never cancels a success. A roll can show both.
  • Teamwork: Before a character rolls, a helper can roll a fitting Attribute + Skill (in combat, this costs the helper 1 action). With at least 1 success, the main roll gets +1d; on an Opportunity, +2d. If the helper's roll shows a Complication, the main roll suffers it too. Helpers give at most +2d to a single roll in total.
  • Specialties: +1d or more when one applies (see Specialties).
  • Fixed difficulty: When there's no opposing roll, the roll needs a number of successes: 2 is a hard task, 3 is a very hard one.

Modifiers

Situational bonuses and penalties to dice pools. They add together; if they bring a pool to 0 dice or fewer, roll a chance die.

  • Advantage / disadvantage: +1d / −1d, for anything that helps or hinders a roll and has no rule of its own.

Light

Rolls that rely on sight take −1d in dim light and −3d in darkness. In total darkness (no light at all, or unnatural darkness a setting defines), you're effectively Blinded: rolls that rely on sight use a chance die.

Cover and concealment

  • Cover: Something solid blocks part of you. Ranged attacks against you take −1d. With full cover, you can't be targeted.
  • Obscured: Smoke, fog or shadow makes you Obscured: rolls to see or target you take −1d, and you can try to hide with Stealth. Deeper darkness uses the Light rules.

Position

  • Prone: melee attacks against you get +1d; ranged attacks against you get −1d.
  • Difficult terrain: −1d to Physical rolls, including attacks and defense rolls, while you stand in it (see Terrain and forced movement).
  • Bracing: the Brace action gives +1d to your defense rolls while you're in cover.

Contested pools

When an effect is "contested by" a pool, the target rolls the one that fits:

AgainstContested by
Feints, tricks, lies, mental attacksIntelligence + Resolve
Pressure, taunts, intimidation, fearWits + Composure
Physical effects that aren't attacks (shoves, poisons, traps)Dexterity + Stamina
Noticing something hidden (a sneaking character, a concealed trap)Wits + Composure

Extended actions

An extended action is a task that takes many rolls over time: researching in an archive, repairing a machine, forging papers, tracking someone across open country.

The GM sets:

  • Pool: the Attribute + Skill that fits, as usual.
  • Target: the total successes needed.
  • Interval: how long each roll takes (minutes, hours or days).
TaskTargetRolls with a 6-die pool
Quick3about 2
Standard6about 3
Long10about 5
Major15about 8
  • Rolling: each roll adds its successes to the total. When the total reaches the target, the task is done.
  • Opportunity: choose a benefit as usual, such as finishing this interval in half the time, saving materials, or a useful discovery. An Opportunity never adds successes to the total.
  • Complication: the GM adds a setback that fits: lose 1 success already gained, spend extra time or materials, or draw attention.
  • No successes: take 1 Stress to press on, or the attempt fails. Trying again usually needs a new approach, new tools or new information. (During downtime, no Stress is paid: the work stops until a later Project action.)
  • Pushing, the stakes die and specialties work on each roll as normal, except that downtime rolls can't be pushed.
  • Teamwork works on each roll. If the GM agrees the task can be split (searching an archive, but not forging one document), several characters can instead work in parallel: each rolls their own pool every interval, and all their successes count toward the target.
  • Deadlines: the GM can set one ("before the curfew bell"). If the target isn't reached in time, the task fails or only partly succeeds.
  • Crafting and repair are extended actions with their own targets and upgrades (see Crafting).
  • Contested extended actions chases, races, a long debate both sides roll each interval, and the first to reach the target wins. Ties go to the side that started it (the pursuer, the challenger).
