Turns and actions

Six seconds a round: fast or slow, a handful of actions and one reaction.

  • Rounds: a round is about 6 seconds of in-world time.
  • Fast and slow turns: Each player chooses at the start of every round. The round runs: fast PCs (2 actions), fast NPCs (2 actions), slow PCs (3 actions), slow NPCs (3 actions). Within a phase, players choose their own order. The GM assigns NPC speeds.
  • Once per turn: Each action type can be taken once per turn, so one attack per turn by default. Talents that allow more say so explicitly.
  • Move 1 action move up to your Speed in meters (see Character Creation). Move is an exception to the once-per-turn rule: you can take it as many times as you have actions, so spending two actions moves you up to twice your Speed.
  • Free actions cost no actions.
  • Reaction: One per round. It refreshes at the start of your turn.
  • Ambush: A character who is unaware of you when you attack or act against them is Surprised until the end of their next turn. A character is unsuspecting while Surprised or unaware of you.

Limited abilities (talents, traits, a player's daily stakes raise) and durations are measured in in-world time.

Set up: Describe how you create an opening, then roll an Attribute + Skill that fits, contested by the target. On a success, you get +1d on your next roll against that target before the end of your next turn. The target defends with the contested pool that fits: Intelligence + Resolve against feints, tricks and studying their weaknesses; Wits + Composure against taunts, pressure and intimidation; Dexterity + Stamina against physical maneuvers.

First aid: Treat an adjacent character, or yourself at −1d. Roll Dexterity + Medicine with the stakes die, needing 1 success. A medical kit gives +1d; with no supplies at all, take −1d.

  • Success: the target stops Bleeding. First aid doesn't heal damage, and an unconscious target stays unconscious (see Waking up).
  • Opportunity: choose a benefit as usual, such as +1d to the next Recover on the target at a rest.
  • Complication: the GM adds a setback that fits, such as using one more charge.

Searching someone

The searcher makes one roll, usually Wits + Investigation. If the person being searched is actively hiding something, it's contested by their Dexterity + Thievery or Subterfuge; otherwise it needs a fixed number of successes set by the GM.

  • Ordinary hidden items are found if the search succeeds.
  • Discreet items need 1 more success: 1 extra success against a fixed difficulty, or at least 1 net success in a contest.

One roll can find the pistol in your coat and still miss the knife in your boot.

Ranges

On a battlemap, one square is 1 meter. Talents and rules use four fixed ranges:

RangeDistanceUsed for
Adjacent1 meter (the next square)Touching, melee, and moving "a short distance"
CloseWithin 5 metersAnything "close to you": commands, aid and most support talents
NearWithin 10 meters (about one Move)Anything "near" a character, shouted orders, small areas
FarBeyond 10 metersSight and ranged weapons
  • A short distance is 1 meter: one adjacent square.
  • A small area is a 3 × 3 meter square (9 squares on a map).
  • Talents can extend ranges. For example, Authority doubles the range of your Leader talents, so their "close" becomes 10 meters.

Terrain and forced movement

  • Difficult terrain rubble, deep mud, a crowd, an entangling trap moving through it costs double, so a Move covers half your Speed there. You take −1d to Physical rolls while standing in it: any roll using a Physical attribute or skill, including attacks and defense rolls (Dodge, Parry or rolled Defense).
  • Dangerous terrain fire, spikes, Dusk-blighted ground terrain that deals damage when you enter it or end your turn in it. The source sets the damage.
  • Pushed back: the effect that pushes you says how far. You move that distance straight away from the source. If something solid stops you early, you fall Prone. Being pushed doesn't provoke Reactive Strikes. Armor can take 1 Wear to ignore a push (see Armor).
