Event tracks

An event track puts something that's coming on the table: reinforcements on the way, an alarm being raised, a plan coming together, a fire spreading. The GM announces the event and draws its track, a row of 2–6 boxes, where the players can see it. When the last box fills, the event happens.

  • Threat tracks are events the characters want to stop. The GM can spend a Complication on a character's roll to fill a box, instead of adding its usual setback. An NPC's Opportunity can fill one the same way.
  • Boon tracks are events the characters want. A player can spend an Opportunity on their roll to fill a box, instead of taking its usual benefit. An NPC's Complication can fill one the same way.
  • Triggers: a track can also name things that fill it (a guard sees you, each curfew bell, each interval of the repair) or clear a box (a convincing lie to a suspicious guard). The GM says them when the track goes up. They apply whatever the dice show.
  • Only rolls that bear on the event can fill its track: an alarm in the house fills from rolls inside the house, not from a brawl in the street outside.
  • One box per result: an Opportunity or a Complication fills one box. A roll that shows both can fill a box on a boon track and a box on a threat track.
  • Stages: a track can name smaller effects at a box before the last ("at 2, the guards grow uneasy").
  • Racing tracks: a threat track and a boon track can run against each other. Whichever fills first decides how the scene turns.
  • When it fills: the event happens and the track ends, unless it says it starts again.
  • Size: about the number of Complications the scene can take: 2–3 for a short scene, 4 for a scene of about a dozen rolls, 6 for a long one.
  • Raising the stakes: Complications come only from the stakes die and from chance dice, so a threat track fills only when the stakes are raised. While one is up, the GM raises the stakes on rolls where its danger is actually present (sneaking past a guard, cracking the safe), not on every roll. With the stakes raised, a box fills about once every three rolls.

Equipment

Everyday things (rope, candles, a blanket, a backpack) aren't tracked: if it makes sense that you have it, you have it. The items below matter at the table. The single-use items (remedies, salves, painkillers, tonics, smelling salts, caltrops, oil flasks and poisons) are counted one by one, unlike other consumables.

ItemEffectBulk
Breakaway clothingFine clothes worn over armor and weapons. While you wear them, your armor counts as Presentable and your weapons as Discreet, but your attacks take −1d and attacks against you get +1d. Tearing them off is a free action. Costs a bag.—
Clothing (ragged, common, fine)+1d to Subterfuge or Socialize to pass as poor, working class or upper class—
Night cloak+1d to Stealth in dim light, darkness or an Obscured area. It can be worn over light armor, not medium or heavy.—
LockOpening it without the key takes Dexterity + Thievery: 1 success for a crude lock, 2 for a good one, 3 for a fine one.—
ManaclesSlipping them takes Dexterity + Thievery, 2 successes; breaking them takes Strength + Athletics, 3 successes.—
CaltropsSingle-use. Scatter them over a small area (1 action): it becomes difficult terrain, and anyone who enters it takes 1 bashing and is Slowed until the end of their next turn. Costs 1 handful.—
Oil flaskThrow it (Dexterity + Ranged, Range 10 m, 1 success to land it where you want): it splashes a small area. Set alight, the area burns as a small fire (1 lethal at the end of each turn in it) for one minute. Single-use.—
PainkillerOne dose, single-use, 1 action to take. For one hour, ignore your Wounded penalty. When it wears off, take 1 Exhausted. A dose costs 1 handful.—
PoisonOne dose, of rating 1 to 5 (see Hazards). Applying it to a blade or a drink takes 1 action; a blade carries it for one hit. A dose costs its rating in handfuls up to rating 3, 5 handfuls at rating 4, a bag at rating 5.—
RemedyOne dose, single-use. At a rest, give it to a patient: they remove 1 more stack of Exhausted, or end Disoriented or Stunned. A dose costs 1 handful.—
SalveOne dose, single-use. At a rest, the patient heals 1 more lethal wound. A dose costs 2 handfuls.—
Smelling saltsSingle-use. Wake up takes 1 action instead of 2 (see Waking up). Costs 1 handful.—
TonicOne dose, single-use. At a rest, clear 1 more Stress; only one tonic per rest. A dose costs 1 handful.—
