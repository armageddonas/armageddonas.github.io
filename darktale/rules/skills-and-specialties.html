Skills

Every character uses the same 24 skills, eight in each category. Skills run 0–5: 1 below average, 2 normally skilled, 3 highly trained, 4 exceptional, 5 the best of the best. Settings can add power skills for characters with access to supernatural powers.

Power skills

A setting's magic, psychic or other supernatural system can add power skills: skills for using that power, such as shaping dreams or bending shadows. The setting defines each one, including which attribute it's usually rolled with.

  • Access: only a character with access to the power can have its power skills. Access comes from whatever the setting says grants the power: a talent, a racial trait, a community trait, or a story event.
  • First dot free: when you gain access, you gain 1 permanent dot in the power skill.
  • Outside the categories: power skills aren't Mental, Physical or Social. They aren't part of the 22 skill dots at creation and don't count against the 11/7/4 split.
  • Improving: raise them through advancement like any other skill, at the same cost.
  • No untrained rolls: without access, you can't roll a power skill at all.
  • Everything else works as usual: specialties, the 0–5 scale, and the rule that talent prerequisites count only permanent dots.

Power pools

Each power has its own pool, which the setting names (Investiture, Mana, Dread…). Its abilities spend from it.

  • Size: 2 + the higher of two attributes the setting names for that power.
  • Spending: each ability says its cost. A sustained ability costs its upkeep each round in combat, or each minute or hour outside it, as the ability says.
  • Running empty: when your pool is empty, you can pay a cost by taking 2 Stress per point. Stress taken this way can overflow like any other (see Stress).
  • Refilling: the setting says how a pool refills. By default, it refills fully at the end of a rest and at the end of a week of downtime.
  • Reserved points: an effect's initial cost stays reserved while the effect is active: a refill doesn't return those points until the effect ends. Upkeep paid to sustain it is spent as usual and isn't reserved. Instant effects reserve nothing. You can end your own effect at any time, as a free action.
  • More than one power: each power keeps its own pool.

Compulsion

Only supernatural powers can decide what a character does. Ordinary effects (threats, persuasion, fear) change dice, Stress and conditions; the player still chooses. When a power compels a target, these rules always apply unless the power says otherwise:

  • Contested: the target resists with Intelligence + Resolve (commands, charms, false loyalties) or Wits + Composure (fear, overwhelming urges), as the power says.
  • One command: the power imposes one short instruction, for the duration the power sets.
  • Broken by harm: the compulsion ends at once if the target is harmed, or is ordered to harm themselves.
  • Breaking free: a compelled character can take 2 Stress to act freely until the end of their turn, but they're Stunned for that turn: in combat, they take a slow turn with one action and no reaction.
  • Aftermath: when it ends, the target knows something was done to them.

Specialties

A specialty is a narrow focus within a skill, written as Skill (Specialty): Weaponry (Swords), Survival (Tracking). The skill tables list examples.

  • Requirement: you need at least 1 permanent dot in a skill to choose or buy a specialty in it.
  • Granted specialties: a specialty granted by a talent or trait ignores that requirement. You gain it even with 0 dots in the skill, and it always gives at least +1d, which offsets part of the untrained penalty. Improving it past +1d still follows the cap below.
  • Bonus: when a specialty applies to a roll, add its bonus: +1d for a new specialty.
  • One per roll: a skill can have several specialties, but only one applies to any roll. Use the one with the highest bonus.
  • Improving a specialty: instead of gaining a new specialty, you can improve one you already have. Each improvement adds +1d to its bonus, up to +3d. A specialty's bonus can't be higher than your permanent dots in its skill. An improvement costs the same as gaining another specialty in that skill (see Advancement).
  • Duplicates: if a talent or trait grants a specialty you already have, improve that specialty by one step instead.
  • Scope: a specialty must be narrower than its skill. Swords, not Melee Weapons; Tracking, not Wilderness. The GM approves each one.
