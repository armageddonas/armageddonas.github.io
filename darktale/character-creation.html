Character Creation

How to build a character. Setting files add their own races and communities (see the settings folder). How characters grow afterwards is on the Advancement page.

Steps

  1. Race and heritage: gain two racial traits.
  2. Community: gain the key trait of the community you grew up in, and put 1 skill dot in one of its skills.
  3. Vocation: gain the vocation's key talent, and put 1 skill dot in one of the vocation's skills.
  4. Attributes: distribute attribute dots.
  5. Skills, specialties and a free pick: spend the rest of your skill dots, choose three specialties, then take one extra talent or community trait.
  6. Derived values and gear: work out Health, Tenacity, Stress, Defense and the rest, and pick starting equipment.

At the end, a starting character has 2 racial traits, 1 community trait (the key trait), 1 talent (the vocation's key talent), three specialties, and one free pick (a talent or a community trait).

1. Race and heritage

Your race is what you are. Your heritage is another bloodline in you: a parent of a different race, or old ancestry that has resurfaced generations later.

Each race has racial traits: a primary trait and usually a secondary trait.

  • Choose a race and take its primary trait. Then choose another or the same race as your heritage and take its secondary trait.
  • Some races have only a primary trait. Their blood is dominant: anyone with that bloodline is of that race. They can be chosen as a race but not as a heritage, so a character of that race always takes a different race as their heritage.
  • Settings add their own races. The core rules include humans.

Human

Driven

Primary trait

When you push yourself, you gain +3d instead of +2d.

Fortune's Favor

Secondary trait

You can raise the stakes on your own rolls twice per day instead of once.

In Duskworld

Human

Humans are found throughout Mendris and much of Eladria. They come in many shapes and forms, but their greatest strength is adaptability. They thrive in communities, use whatever resources are available, and have repeatedly survived circumstances that should have destroyed them.

Humans use the racial traits in the core rules (Driven and Fortune's Favor).

Kehar Duskworld

Kehars are said to have been born from dreams, and a rare few can shape their dreams into reality. In a world that is steadily losing its color, kehars remain vibrant and colorful. Their appearance and culture vary between communities.

In Mendris, kehars are sometimes regarded with suspicion: some believe they're still connected to the krens because of what their ancestors did. As a result, kehars often form close-knit communities and rely heavily on one another. In the Frontiers, generations of shared hardship mean they're usually treated like their human neighbors.

Lucid

Primary trait

To a kehar, a bad outcome is only a dream that can be dreamed again. After you roll, you can reroll any number of dice that didn't succeed, taking 1 Stress per die. You can't reroll the stakes die, or a chance die that rolled a 1.

Dreaming Calm

Secondary trait

A part of every kehar is always dreaming, and the waking world weighs on them a little less. Once per hour, you can become Determined (your next Stress cost is reduced by 1).

Shanat Duskworld

Shanats descend from humans and kehars touched by kren blood. Their ancestry is complicated, and most shanats don't discuss their origins openly.

The blood manifests in different ways: some shanats develop supernatural abilities tied to aspects of darkness, while others show only subtle physical peculiarities. Kren blood is feared in Mendris, and using its powers is forbidden, so shanats who have them use them in secret.

The Shantari, one of the Great Houses, are a shanat family. Their kren connection passed down the old codes: secret codes and keys that let them read and run kren factories and machines that remain a mystery to the rest of Mendris. This knowledge is taught, not inherited: a shanat raised outside the family doesn't have it.

Shanat blood is dominant. A child with any Shanat parent is Shanat, and the bloodline never shows up as a mere heritage. Every Shanat still carries a human or kehar bloodline, from a parent or from older ancestry resurfacing.

Shanat have only a primary trait. A Shanat character always takes a different race as their heritage, and Shanat can't be chosen as a heritage.

Night Sight

Primary trait

You ignore dice penalties from dim light and darkness, but not from total darkness. The deep Dusklands count as total darkness.

2. Community

A community is a group of people you belong to: where you live, who looks out for you, and what that life teaches. At character creation, you choose the community you grew up in. Communities are defined by each setting.

Each community is a small tree of community traits, read like a vocation's talent tree (see Talents):

  • Key trait: every member of that community gains it.
  • Further traits: one or two more, each with an after tag and other prerequisites (skills, attributes, race).
  • At character creation, you gain your community's key trait. Its further traits come later through advancement, or you can take one with your free pick in step 5.
  • Community skills: each community lists two or three skills. Put 1 skill dot in one of them.

Key traits are about as strong as a secondary racial trait, and lean toward social and story hooks. A further trait costs the same as a talent, at creation and in advancement.

Frontier Folk Duskworld

The people of the fortified farmsteads around Dawnkeep: working the fields, standing watch, and living close to the edge of the Dusklands.

Community skills
Survival, Athletics or Animal Ken.
Starting gear
Work tools, a lantern, and travel papers for the Frontiers.
Key trait

Farmstead Kin

The farmsteads look after their own. You count as having a patron among Frontier folk, and at any farmstead you can find food, shelter and a sympathetic ear.

Gentry Duskworld

The administrators, officials and old families of Dawnkeep's prominent Sectors.

Community skills
Politics, Academics or Persuasion.
Starting gear
Fine clothes, full travel papers, and firearm permission: when your vocation gives you a weapon, you can take a pistol or rifle for one of them.
Key trait

Sector Standing

You count as having a patron in your Sector's circles, and in high society. Clerks, officials and guards treat you as someone who matters: you can get an audience, a permit or a hearing that others would wait weeks for.

Guildfolk Duskworld

The laborers, artisans and guild members of the working Sectors that keep Dawnkeep running: factories, workshops, canals and markets.

Community skills
Crafts, Athletics or Streetwise.
Starting gear
A tool kit, a guild token, and travel papers for the working Sectors.
Key trait

Guild Ties

You count as having a patron among the laborers, guilds and unions of the working Sectors. You know how to find work, a bed for the night, or someone who owes the guild a favor.
Guildfolkafter Guild Ties

Stamina 3+
Years of hard labor made you tough. Your Beaten Down threshold is 1 higher.

Street Sense

Gain a Streetwise (Dawnkeep) specialty. You always know the fastest way through the working Sectors, and you get +1d to notice when you're being followed or cheated.

Dusklanders Duskworld

The few communities beyond the Beacons that survive in the Dusklands through magic and sacrifice. People who live in the Dusklands are called Duskroamers.

Community skills
Survival, Occult or Stealth.
Starting gear
A wardwright charm (+1d to Endure the Dusk) and Dusklands clothing. No travel papers.

Born under the Dusk: if you grew up among the Dusklanders, you start play with Dusking 1 and the Grey-Weaned Dusk trait (see Born under the Dusk). Every curse test in Mendris finds it.

Key trait

Beyond the Beacons

You count as having a patron among the Dusklands communities, and they treat you as one of their own. You know their safe paths and hidden refuges, and where to find shelter in the Dusklands.
Dusklandersafter Beyond the Beacons

Eye for the Strange

Intelligence 3+
You get +1d to identify dusktouched creatures, mutations and Dusk phenomena. On a success, you also learn one weakness or danger.

Dusk Sense

Wits 3+
You feel the Dusk. You know an area's exposure level before you enter it, and you can't be Surprised by dusktouched or duskborn creatures.

The Wards Duskworld

Those who passed through an institution rather than a family: an orphanage, a workhouse, a parish home, or a military school.

Community skills
Streetwise, Thievery or Academics.
Starting gear
One kit of your choice (your institution taught a trade), worn clothes, and papers from your institution.
Key trait

Fellow Wards

You count as having a patron among the others who passed through your institution. They're scattered across Mendris, in every kind of position, and they remember where they came from.

The merchants and drivers of the caravans that carry goods between the settlements of Mendris, always on the move.

Joining another community

Characters can join other communities during play, the way they can train into another vocation.

  • Gain its key trait through advancement, at the cost of a talent. After that, its further traits are open to you like your own community's.
  • No skill dot. The community skill dot is only for the community you grew up in.
  • You keep your first community. Joining a new one adds to who you are; it doesn't replace it.

The cost is the only requirement, but joining a community should mean something in the story. GMs are encouraged to work with the player on an in-world reason and a chance to roleplay it: a season travelling with a caravan, a debt repaid to a guild, a winter sheltered on a Frontier farmstead.

A character can also start play as a member of a second community, using the free pick in step 5. Their backstory covers how they came to belong to it.

3. Vocation

Your vocation is what you do: your calling and your training. Vocations are in the core rules: Agent, Envoy, Hunter, Leader, Scholar and Warrior.

  • Gain your vocation's key talent. Other talents come through advancement, or with your free pick in step 5.
  • Vocation skills: each vocation lists three skills. Put 1 skill dot in one of them.

4. Attributes

  • All nine attributes start at 1.
  • Rank the Mental, Physical and Social categories. Put 5 dots in the first, 4 in the second and 3 in the third.
  • Cap at creation: no attribute can have more than 4 dots. A 5th dot comes only through advancement.

5. Skills, specialties and a free pick

Skills

  • You have 22 skill dots, split by category: rank Mental, Physical and Social, and put 11, 7 and 4 dots in them.
  • The community and vocation dots from steps 2 and 3 are part of these 22. Each comes out of the pool for its skill's category, so you have 20 left to spend freely.
  • The community and vocation dots can go in the same skill.
  • Cap at creation: no skill can have more than 3 permanent dots. Higher ratings come through advancement.
  • Temporary dots from talents (such as the Scholar's Erudition) don't count toward the cap, so they can take a skill above 3.

Specialties

Choose three specialties (see Specialties for the rules and examples):

  • One from your community: in the skill where you put your community dot.
  • One from your vocation: in the skill where you put your vocation dot.
  • One free: in any skill with at least 1 permanent dot.

If your community and vocation dots went in the same skill, take two different specialties in it. All three start at +1d; specialties can only be improved through advancement.

Free pick

Once your skills are set, take one of the following. Its prerequisites must be met.

  • Another talent from your vocation, to go further along one of its trees.
  • A second vocation's key talent, to start play with two vocations.
  • A further trait of your community.
  • A second community's key trait, to start play as a member of two communities. Your backstory explains how you came to belong to it. You don't get its skill dot.

The free pick comes after attributes and skills, so a talent or trait that requires either can be taken as long as your final ratings meet it. With the skill cap at 3, talents that require a skill of 4+ can't be taken at creation.

6. Derived values and gear

ValueFormula
Size5 for an adult of any core race. Races or effects can change it.
HealthSize + Stamina
TenacityResolve + Composure + 3
StressCurrent Tenacity + 2
DefenseThe lower of Dexterity and Wits
Beaten Down thresholdStamina + Resolve
SpeedStrength + Dexterity + Size + species factor, in meters

Speed is how far you can move with one move action. The species factor is 0 by default; a setting can give a race or creature a different one (for example, +2 for a fast runner, −2 for a slow one). Because Size is part of it, smaller characters are slower and larger ones are faster.

Starting gear

You start with:

  • Your community's gear: clothes, everyday items and anything the setting ties to it (listed with each community).
  • Your vocation's gear: weapons, armor and a kit (listed with each vocation).
  • 2 handfuls of wealth (see Wealth).

Only the first vocation and the community you grew up in give gear; a second vocation or community from the free pick doesn't. Settings can add rules about who may carry what (Duskworld: firearms).

Wealth

Money is abstract: nobody counts coins. Wealth comes in three units, each worth 10 of the one below it.

UnitWorthWhat it buys
Handful—A night at an inn, a bribe to a clerk, a set of tools
Bag10 handfulsA good weapon, light or medium armor, passage with a caravan, a serious bribe
Chest10 bagsHeavy armor, a horse and wagon, a favor from the powerful

Each setting names the units to fit its world (Duskworld: handfuls of boxings, slabs and shells; see the Duskworld Overview). The rules always use handful, bag and chest.

  • On your sheet: 9 handful boxes, 9 bag boxes, and a count of chests. When you'd mark a 10th handful, clear your handfuls and mark a bag instead; bags roll up into a chest the same way.
  • Paying: clear the boxes for the price. If you don't have enough of a smaller unit, break a bigger one: clear 1 bag and mark 10 handfuls, then pay.
  • Small purchases: anything worth less than a handful (a meal, a drink, a fare) isn't tracked.
  • Selling: you get about half the price back, at the GM's call.
  • Restocking: Prepare restocks consumables for free. In a settlement, each extra rest action costs 2 handfuls, so you can Prepare again to refill more kit charges (see Rest).
  • Rewards: an ordinary job pays a handful or a few; a big job pays a bag. Chests are story rewards, not wages.
In Duskworld

Glints and boxings

The money of Mendris comes from glints: cube-like sources of power built by the krens. There are a great many of them, but no one can make true glints. Mendris's workshops make weaker copies, forged glints; the true kren glints are found only in the abandoned kren factories of the Dusklands. Glints also power every Device (see Glints and Devices).

Mendris's currency is cut from empty glints. The cut pieces are called boxings. Duskworld names the three wealth units after them:

UnitDuskworld nameWorth
HandfulA handful of boxingsEveryday money
BagA slab: a large uncut piece of an empty glint10 handfuls of boxings
ChestA shell: a whole empty glint, never cut10 slabs

Most people only ever handle boxings. Merchants and caravans deal in slabs. Shells belong to the Houses and the trading companies.
