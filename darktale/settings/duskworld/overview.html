Duskworld: Eladria

The world of Eladria, slowly being consumed by the Dusk. Races are on the Races page; the Dusk's rules are on The Dusk. The player-facing introduction is the primer.

History

Once, the world was full of light and color. Then Eladria succumbed to the Dusk and the grey.

Long ago, humans and kehars lived throughout Eladria, mostly in peace. But some kehars sought powers beyond what the world could offer, and strange beings from the unknown answered them: the krens. Their gifts became corruption, and the krens spread across the world, turning Eladria into a realm of demons and monsters.

During the Era of Sorrow, the people of Eladria were enslaved and hunted. Humans were gathered into camps and prisons and sacrificed in dark rituals. Kehars fled to the fringes of civilization and hid from their new masters.

When all seemed lost, the Light appeared. The Allmother manifested from the hopes and prayers of the people and drove back the krens. The survivors pushed the krens far enough away to found Dawnkeep, the first Free City.

The victory didn't last. The Allmother's First Followers, unsatisfied with the power she had given them, murdered her. The act stripped them of what remained of their humanity, and they still wander somewhere as shells of their former selves.

With the Allmother's death, the Dusk came.

The Dusk

The Dusk isn't simply darkness. It corrupts reality itself.

  • Where it spreads, the world loses its color. Light dims, the land withers, and magic and runetech turn unpredictable: wards flicker, silos spoil their grain, and machines run harder, weaker, or do things their makers never meant.
  • It twists living things. Plants mutate, animals become monstrous, and people exposed to it for too long may lose their minds or their humanity.
  • Few understand what the Dusk is, or why it came with the Allmother's death.

The Dusklands

The lands touched by the Dusk. The ground is dark grey, the vegetation black and twisted, and everything that survives has changed. Plants may grow enormous, grow strange materials in place of wood, or gain rudimentary sentience.

  • Dusktouched: animals and people changed by the Dusk's corruption.
  • Krens and their creations still roam the wilds.
  • Duskborn: powerful horrors born from the Dusk itself.

Some people live in the Dusklands, surviving alongside the monsters, protected from corruption by magic and sacrifice. They're called Duskroamers.

Mendris

One of the last great bastions of civilization: the capital Dawnkeep and the fortified farmsteads around it, the Frontiers. The Great Houses hold the real power.

  • Beacons: massive towers that combine the Allmother's Light with ancient kren technology. They keep the Dusk away from Dawnkeep and the farmsteads.
  • Rigid society: social status is hard to change. Farmsteaders stay farmsteaders and cityfolk stay cityfolk. In Dawnkeep, your Sector decides much of your standing, your work and your circles.
  • Controlled travel: movement between regions is tightly controlled, for order and for protection from what lies beyond the Beacons.

The Frontiers

Simple, communal and dangerous. Most people work the fields or serve in the security forces that protect the settlements. Others keep the farmsteads running: runesmiths, wardwrights, artisans, hunters, and engineers who maintain the Beacons, machinery and grain silos. The silos use runetech to preserve and purify food, so the Frontiers are largely self-sufficient.

Humans and kehars in the Frontiers lead similar lives. After generations of shared hardship, kehars are usually treated like their human neighbors, though old prejudices remain.

Dawnkeep

Broader, wealthier and more complicated. A city of rivers, canals, factories, markets, trains, Beacons and ancient technology, divided into Sectors with their own character and hierarchy.

  • Most residents are laborers or artisans.
  • Those born in prominent Sectors have better access to administrative, clerical, logistical and governmental positions.
  • With wealth, connections or the right family name, people rise further: merchants who travel across Mendris, shareholders in trading companies, members of the institutions that control the city.

The world is dying, but Dawnkeep is still alive.

Travel papers

Movement between regions of Mendris is controlled, and travel papers say where you may go. Most people only hold papers for their own region: Frontier folk for the Frontiers, guildfolk for the working Sectors, caravaners for the trade routes. Gentry hold full papers. Duskroamers have none.

  • Checkpoints guard Beacon gates, Sector boundaries, train stations and the roads between settlements.
  • Without the right papers, you're turned back, or detained if you're caught somewhere you shouldn't be. Ways around it: a bribe (a handful, more for a serious checkpoint), forged papers (an extended action with Academics or Expression, helped by a writing case), sneaking past (Stealth), or a patron who vouches for you.
  • Firearms are legal only for Gentry, the Houses and the security forces. Anyone else caught with one faces the same trouble as having no papers, or worse. See Runetech.

Life in Eladria

Everyone knows civilization lives on borrowed time. The Beacons hold back the Dusk, the Frontiers provide food, hunters venture into the Dusklands for resources, soldiers protect the settlements, artisans maintain the machinery, and merchants move goods between distant communities.

Beyond all of it lies the Dusk. Some stay behind the walls. Others venture beyond them.

The Allmother's mantra. Said at every curfew bell: "Her light is our home. Her light protects."

Institutions and places (working canon)

Established through play. Treat as a strong starting point rather than fixed canon.

  • House Callarain: one of the Great Houses. Its engineers' guild builds and maintains Beacon technology and controls apprenticeships into the trade.
  • House Shantari: one of the Great Houses, a shanat family. Its kren blood passed down the old codes that let them read and run kren factories nobody else understands.
  • The Sixth House (the Syndicate): an underworld power beside, or beneath, the official Houses. Crime, debts, favors and leverage; it hires people for jobs it can't be seen doing.
  • The Gutterlord: a crime lord of the Fork, with an estate where he holds boxing nights.
  • The IEE: investigates and reports on deaths, disappearances and accidents across Mendris. Thorough when the people involved are well connected; thin or silent when the missing are poor, or broke curfew.
  • Curfew: settlements ring a curfew bell at dusk. People who go out after it often don't come back. Whether that's the Dusk reaching past the Beacons or something else is unknown; it's treated as grim common knowledge, not superstition.
  • The curse of the Dusk: a recognized affliction from prolonged exposure or corruption, distinct from being taken outright. It can be formally tested for. Those who fail, or are suspected, are usually sent somewhere like Brightgate. In the rules, the curse is the Dusking Scar.
  • Taken by the Dusk: any death that leaves no sign of physical harm, when the victim had the curse, is said to be the Dusk taking them.
  • Brightgate: a mental institution in a rehabilitated old mansion, where people suspected of the curse or deemed unwell are confined, sometimes for years. It's said to hold more restless presences than it was meant to treat.
  • The Fork: a poor, rough district of tenements, workhouses and gang territory, where parish churches do more good than the authorities. Smaller supporting Beacons hold it against the Dusk.
  • Southbank: Dawnkeep's docklands. Trade, smuggling, and the place goods (sometimes whole convoys) go missing before anyone official notices.
