Setting · Duskworld

Eladria

What was once a world of light and color has now succumbed to the Dusk and the grey.

Duskworld
PrimerOverviewRunetechThe DimmingDuskworld rules

Long ago, humans and kehars shared Eladria in uneasy peace, until a handful of kehars reached for power the world couldn't give them and something answered. The krens came. What began as gifts became corruption, and for a hundred years, remembered as the Era of Sorrow, Eladria belonged to them: people enslaved, hunted, sacrificed, while kehars fled to the fringes and hid.

Then the Allmother rose out of the last of humanity's hope and drove the krens back far enough to raise Dawnkeep, the first Free City. Her own First Followers murdered her for more power than she'd give them, an act so vile it left them wandering shells of themselves, still out there somewhere. In her place, the Dusk came.

The Dusk

The Dusk isn't simple darkness. It's corruption that spreads through reality itself. Wherever it reaches, color drains out, the land withers, and magic and runetech turn unpredictable: a ward flickers, a silo spoils its grain, a machine runs harder, weaker, or does something its makers never meant.

Stay in it too long and it changes you, if it doesn't kill you first. Plants grow strange and enormous, animals turn monstrous, people lose pieces of their mind, or themselves. The ruined land past the Beacons is called the Dusklands. People do live out there, the Duskroamers, protected by magic and sacrifice, alongside the dusktouched the corruption has made, the duskborn horrors the Dusk gave rise to on its own, and the krens, who never really left. Nobody knows what the Dusk is, or why it came the moment the Allmother died.

Where You Live

What's left of civilization calls itself Mendris: Dawnkeep, the last great capital, and the Frontiers, the fortified farmsteads that keep it fed. Dawnkeep was raised by the Allmother herself, and the towering Beacons shining with her golden light are the only reason the city, or anything else in Mendris, still stands untouched by the Dusk.

Survival came at a cost. Where you're born mostly decides how you live: cityfolk stay cityfolk, farmsteaders stay farmsteaders, and inside Dawnkeep your Sector shapes your work, your standing and everyone you're likely to ever meet. The Great Houses hold the real power. Travel is tightly controlled, not just for order, but because almost nowhere past the walls is safe to wander into.

What Everyone Knows

  • The curfew bell. Every settlement rings it at dusk. People who go out after it often don't come back. Whether that's the Dusk reaching past the Beacons or something else, nobody says out loud.
  • Papers, please. Your travel papers say where you're allowed to be, and checkpoints guard Beacon gates, Sector lines and train stations. Without the right papers you're turned back, or detained. Bribes, forgeries and friends in the right places all help. Only the Gentry, the Houses and the security forces may carry firearms.
  • The curse test. Too long in the Dusk leaves a mark, and Mendris can test for it. Fail, or just fall under suspicion, and you may be sent somewhere like Brightgate, an old mansion turned asylum where people are kept for years. When someone with the curse dies without a mark on them, everyone says the Dusk took them.
  • Boxings. Money is cut from glints, kren-made cubes of power that nobody can truly make anymore; the workshops of Mendris only manage weaker copies. Most people only ever handle a handful of boxings; merchants deal in slabs, and the Houses in whole shells.

Who You Might Be

Humans are found everywhere in Mendris, with no single gift so much as a talent for making do, building communities, and surviving circumstances that should have ended them, again and again.

Kehars are said to be born from dreams; a rare few can shape their dreams into reality. In a world losing its color, kehars keep theirs, and it makes them easy to spot. For some in Mendris, it also makes them easy to distrust for what their ancestors once did.

Shanats descend from humans and kehars touched by kren blood. They've inherited strange abilities tied to darkness and small physical peculiarities. In the Shantari family, one of the Great Houses, that kren connection also passed down the old codes that let them read and run kren factories nobody else understands. Kren blood is feared in Mendris, and using its powers is forbidden, so shanats who have them use them in secret.

Our Story: The Dimming

One of the smaller Beacons that holds the Fork has been dimming for weeks. Its light flickers after curfew, the grey is creeping into the alleys at its edge, and the people who live beneath it have started sleeping with the lamps lit. If it goes out, the Dusk walks into the Fork.

A repair device was sent to fix it. It never arrived. The Gutterlord, a crime lord of the Fork, had it stolen, and nobody official is going to march into his estate to take it back.

That's where you come in.

Part one: Fight night

The Gutterlord is throwing a boxing night at the back of his estate. Half the Fork's underworld and a few slumming Gentry will be there, the betting will be loud, and every eye will be on the ring. It's the best chance you'll get to slip in, find the device and walk out with it.

Part two: Into the grey

Getting the device out is only half the job. It still has to reach the failing Beacon, and the streets beneath a dying light aren't the city you know. The Dusk is already leaning on the Fork. Someone has to carry the device through it and make the repair before the light goes out.

Make a character with a reason to take a job like this: a debt, a home under that Beacon, a grudge against the Gutterlord, or just the pay. The party will need people who can blend into a crowd, get past locks and guards, understand Beacon machinery, and keep their heads when the grey closes in.

Where You Fit

None of this means much until it's yours. A few places to start:

The Frontier Hand

Grew up working fields in sight of a Beacon that never once felt fully reliable.

The Fork Courier

Knows every Sector's shortcuts, and none of its secrets, yet.

The Dusklands Hunter

Has gone further out than most, and come back... mostly right.

The Clerk

Knows which form gets you through a checkpoint, and which one makes someone disappear.

The Parish Ward

Raised by a Fork parish home, and still owes the sisters, and the streets, more than they'd like.

The Caravaner

Has carried things through Southbank that were never on the manifest.

Or none of the above. Eladria's big enough for a character the table hasn't thought of yet.

The Table's Tone

Eladria is bleak, but Dawnkeep's whole existence is proof that bleak isn't the same as hopeless. People still have dreams and hopes. The Dusk has been there for everyone's entire life, a threat to avoid rather than a reminder of what came before; for most people, life before the Dusk is just a fairytale.

Expect the Dusk to show up as body horror at the edges, madness that can turn characters against themselves or their friends, prejudice as a real and sometimes ugly social force, and no easy answers about who gets protected and who doesn't.

If any of that isn't for you, or you just want to know where the table's lines are, ask before we start. That's what session zero is for.

The ruined world beyond the Beacons' reach.

Dawnkeep and the Frontiers: one of the last great strongholds of civilization.
