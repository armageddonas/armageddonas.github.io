  • Knuckles are worn, not held: attacks with them are unarmed attacks (Strength + Brawl) that deal their damage.
  • Improvised weapons (a bottle, a chair leg, a lamp) use the statistics of the nearest weapon in the table and gain Fragile.

Weapon traits

  • Blast: A hit also deals 1 damage, one severity lower, to everyone else in a small area around the target. No one pays Stress for it. On an Opportunity, they all take the full hit instead.
  • Dangerous: When an attack with it shows a Complication, the GM can spend it to have you graze an ally within its reach or range.
  • Deadly: An Opportunity on the attack adds +1 lethal damage.
  • Defensive: You can Brace while holding it, even without cover.
  • Discreet: When someone searches you, finding this item takes 1 more success than finding anything else (see Searching someone).
  • Ensnare: A hit deals no damage: the target is Immobilized and Prone until freed. Freeing them takes 1 action and Strength + Athletics, 2 successes, by them or anyone adjacent.
  • Fragile: When an attack with it shows a Complication, the GM can spend it to break the weapon.
  • Fuse: Lighting it takes 1 action. It goes off at the end of a round you choose, 1 to 6 rounds later, or on impact if you throw it.
  • Heavy: Needs Strength 3; −1d per missing dot.
  • Knockdown: On an Opportunity, the target falls Prone.
  • Light: Small and one-handed. Usable while Grappled.
  • Loaded X: The weapon holds X shots. Each attack spends 1; with none left, it can't fire until it's reloaded. Reloading to full takes 1 action, or none outside combat. When an attack with it shows a Complication, the GM can spend it to leave the weapon with only 1 shot, if it has more.
  • Piercing X: Ignores X points of armor rating.
  • Precise: On called shots, 6s explode.
  • Quickdraw: Drawing or stowing it is a free action.
  • Range: Attacks beyond it take −2d, up to double the range.
  • Reach: Attack a target up to 2 meters away.
  • Shield (X): +X dice to Parry while held. With Shield (2) or higher, it also counts as cover against ranged attacks.
  • Single-use: Spent after one attack.
  • Stunning: On an Opportunity, the target is Stunned until the end of their next turn.
  • Throwable: Can be thrown with Dexterity + Ranged, Range 10 m.
  • Two-handed: Needs both hands, so you can't hold a shield, a second weapon or a light source while attacking with it.
  • Versatile: Can be used one- or two-handed; +1 damage when used two-handed.

In polite company

Visible weapons and armor don't belong at a party, a market, a temple, or anywhere people don't expect a fight. There, visible weapons give −1d to Social rolls and armor gives −2d, whatever its weight (−3d for both), and either draws attention, often the watch's. Discreet weapons kept out of sight don't, and neither does armor with the Presentable trait: armor that passes for ordinary clothes.

Armor

  • Armor rating: When an attack hits or grazes you, subtract your armor rating from the damage. Damage that gets through keeps its type. If armor absorbs all of it, you still take 1 bashing damage.
  • Wear: Armor has a Wear track with boxes equal to its current rating. It takes 1 Wear when a hit gets past it (damage beyond the 1-bashing floor) or when the attack scores an Opportunity, at most 1 Wear per hit. You can also have your armor take 1 Wear to ignore one condition a hit would cause, such as Prone, Bleeding or Stunned, or being pushed back.
  • Battered: When the Wear track fills, the armor's rating drops by 1. A full Wear track takes no more Wear from hits. You can still choose to have it take Wear to ignore a condition; each point that doesn't fit lowers the rating by 1 more. Prepare at a rest clears Wear. If a rest ends with the Wear track still full, the rating drops by 1 more. Lost rating comes back only when the armor is repaired during downtime.
  • Broken: Armor reduced to rating 0 is broken. It gives no protection until repaired during downtime.

Kits

A kit is a set of tools for one kind of work. It gives +1d to the rolls it covers. Working with no proper tools at all gives −1d.

  • Charges: a kit has 3 charges. Spend 1 each time you take its +1d. On a Complication, the GM can spend it to use up 1 more charge.
  • Empty kit: at 0 charges you still have the tools (no −1d), but you get no bonus.
  • Refilling: each Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge on each of your kits. In a settlement you can buy extra rest actions (see Rest) to Prepare again. A week of downtime refills every kit to full.

Everyday things (rope, candles, a blanket, a backpack) aren't tracked: if it makes sense that you have it, you have it. The items below matter at the table. Every item has a bulk from 1 to 3, or no bulk at all. Add up the bulk of everything you carry, including worn armor, to find your load.

Companions

A companion is an animal that works with you. It's an NPC with a rating (1–5) and rolls like one: double its rating for what it's proficient in, its rating for what it knows, and a chance die for everything else.

  • Health: Size + rating. It heals like a character; Recover on an animal uses Animal Handling instead of Medicine.
  • No Stress: it counts as having a full Stress track, so it can't pay Stress costs.
  • Defense: it rolls its rating to defend, like a minion. It doesn't Dodge or Parry.
  • Acting: it has no turn of its own. On your turn, you can spend your own actions to have it act. Talents such as Animal Bond give it actions for free.
  • Orders: outside a fight, unusual or dangerous orders need an Animal Handling roll.
  • Getting one: Animal Bond gives a rating 2 animal of your choice: one from the table, or another animal you and the GM build at rating 2. Otherwise, a trained animal costs a bag (a wolfhound, 2 bags).

Devices

A Device is a complex machine: a trap, an alarm, a launcher, an instrument, an automaton. It needs a core component and the skill to build it (below), and has a rating from 1 to 5 and a single function. Otherwise it's used like any item: you pick it up, aim it, set it or switch it on, and roll your own pool when its function calls for a roll. Ordinary weapons, armor and tools aren't Devices: make them as ordinary items and improve them with upgrades. What powers a Device depends on the setting: clockwork, alchemy, runes, lightning, or something stranger.

  • Using one: setting, starting or using a Device takes 1 action unless it says otherwise. When its function calls for a roll, you roll the pool it names.
  • Automated Devices work on their own once they're set or started, and are marked Automated. When one makes a roll, it rolls twice its rating in dice, like an NPC rolling something it's proficient in (see NPCs), and your skill doesn't matter.
  • Rating sets a Device's charges, its scale (range, area, duration, people affected, as its function says), how hard it is to disable, and, for an Automated Device, its dice.
  • Charges: a Device has charges equal to its rating. Each use spends 1. Each Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge, like a kit. At 0 charges it stops working until it's refilled; only a Device that says it's single-use is spent for good.
  • Burning the core: before a roll a Device makes or powers, you can burn out its core component: the roll gets +2d and raises the stakes, and the core is destroyed. The Device doesn't work again until a new core is fitted.
  • Who can build one: Devices are complex work. You need Crafts 3+ and Technology 2+ to build one, and its rating can't be higher than your Crafts dots − 2 (rating 1 at Crafts 3, 2 at Crafts 4, 3 at Crafts 5).
  • Crafting: choose the rating before you start. The target is 6 for rating 1, +3 for each rating above it: 9 at rating 2, 12 at 3, 15 at 4, 18 at 5. Each Device also needs a core component the setting defines, such as a power source or a focus. Without one, the Device can't be built.
  • Disabling or bypassing: roll Dexterity or Intelligence + Technology, contested by twice the Device's rating, whether it's Automated or not. On a success, it's disarmed, jammed or slipped past; on a failure, it triggers or alerts its owner, as fits the Device.
  • Price: by rating, not counting the core component (table below). Materials for crafting one are half that, as for any item.
  • Settings add their own Devices, and decide what core components are and where to find them. Higher-rated Devices can also be found, bought or salvaged.

Glints

Glints are the core component of every Duskworld Device. Each glint has a rating from 1 to 5, which is its number of charges. The Device's own rating still sets its dice and the crafting limit, and any glint fits any Device.

GlintRatingWhere it comes fromRefill
Forged glint1–3Mendris's workshops, copying kren techEach Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge
Kren glint4–5Storage compartments in the abandoned kren factories of the DusklandsRefills to full at the end of each rest
  • Charges: a Device spends 1 charge from its glint each time it's used. The GM can spend a Complication on a Device roll to drain 1 more.
  • Running dry: a glint at 0 charges stops powering its Device until it's refilled. Only a glint that says it's single-use, like the flashstick's, is spent for good.
  • Downtime: the end of a week of downtime refills every glint that isn't burnt out.
  • Burning out: a glint is the core you burn when burning the core: before a roll its Device or runetech item powers, you can burn the glint out for +2d, raising the stakes. The glint cracks, and it's scrap, forged or kren.
  • Refitting: putting a new glint into a Device takes an hour of work and needs Technology 2+.
  • Shells: only the Houses can drain a kren glint without cracking it. The empty, whole glint is a shell, Mendris's largest unit of money, and only House Merovant cuts shells into slabs and boxings.

Buying and trading

Forged glints are legal, and are bought and sold like other gear.

Forged glintPrice

Kren glints are another matter. Selling one is illegal: they belong to the Houses.

  • Surrendering a kren glint to a House earns favors equal to its rating with that House (see Favors).
  • Fencing one gets its rating in slabs, a fraction of what it's worth. Finding a buyer is a Manipulation + Streetwise roll; on a Complication, someone notices.
