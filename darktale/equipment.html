Weapon traits

  • Deadly: An Opportunity on the attack adds +1 lethal damage.
  • Discreet: When someone searches you, finding this item takes 1 more success than finding anything else (see Searching someone).
  • Heavy: Needs Strength 3; −1d per missing dot.
  • Knockdown: On an Opportunity, the target falls Prone.
  • Light: Small and one-handed. Usable while Grappled.
  • Piercing X: Ignores X points of armor rating.
  • Precise: On called shots, 6s explode.
  • Quickdraw: Drawing or stowing it is a free action.
  • Range: Attacks beyond it take −2d, up to double the range.
  • Reach: Attack a target up to 2 meters away.
  • Shield (X): +X dice to Parry while held. With Shield (2) or higher, it also counts as cover against ranged attacks.
  • Slow: Reloading takes 1 action.
  • Stunning: On an Opportunity, the target is Stunned until the end of their next turn.
  • Throwable: Can be thrown with Dexterity + Ranged, Range 10 m.
  • Two-handed: Needs both hands, so you can't hold a shield, a second weapon or a light source while attacking with it.
  • Versatile: Can be used one- or two-handed; +1 damage when used two-handed.

Armor

  • Armor rating: When an attack hits or grazes you, subtract your armor rating from the damage. Damage that gets through keeps its type. If armor absorbs all of it, you still take 1 bashing damage.
  • Wear: Armor has a Wear track with boxes equal to its current rating. It takes 1 Wear when a hit gets past it (damage beyond the 1-bashing floor) or when the attack scores an Opportunity, at most 1 Wear per hit. You can also have your armor take 1 Wear to ignore one condition a hit would cause, such as Prone, Bleeding or Stunned, or being pushed back.
  • Battered: When the Wear track fills, the armor's rating drops by 1. A full Wear track takes no more Wear from hits. You can still choose to have it take Wear to ignore a condition; each point that doesn't fit lowers the rating by 1 more. Prepare at a rest clears Wear. If a rest ends with the Wear track still full, the rating drops by 1 more. Lost rating comes back only when the armor is repaired during downtime.
  • Broken: Armor reduced to rating 0 is broken. It gives no protection until repaired during downtime.

Kits

A kit is a set of tools for one kind of work. It gives +1d to the rolls it covers. Working with no proper tools at all gives −1d.

  • Charges: a kit has 3 charges. Spend 1 each time you take its +1d. A Complication on that roll spends 1 more.
  • Empty kit: at 0 charges you still have the tools (no −1d), but you get no bonus.
  • Refilling: each Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge on each of your kits. In a settlement you can buy extra rest actions (see Rest) to Prepare again. A week of downtime refills every kit to full.

Encumbrance

Every item has a bulk from 1 to 3, or no bulk at all. Add up the bulk of everything you carry, including worn armor, to find your load.

Companions

A companion is an animal that works with you. It's an NPC with a rating (1–5) and rolls like one: double its rating for what it's proficient in, its rating for what it knows, and a chance die for everything else.

  • Health: Size + rating. It heals like a character; Recover on an animal uses Animal Ken instead of Medicine.
  • No Stress: it counts as having a full Stress track, so it can't pay Stress costs.
  • Defense: it rolls its rating to defend, like a minion. It doesn't Dodge or Parry.
  • Acting: it has no turn of its own. On your turn, you can spend your own actions to have it act. Talents such as Animal Bond give it actions for free.
  • Orders: outside a fight, unusual or dangerous orders need an Animal Ken roll.
  • Getting one: Animal Bond gives a rating 2 animal of your choice: one from the table, or another animal you and the GM build at rating 2. Otherwise, a trained animal costs a bag.

Devices

A Device is a self-operating mechanism: a trap, an alarm, a launcher, an automaton. Once it's set or started, it works on its own, so the user's skill doesn't matter. Weapons, armor and tools whose use depends on the wielder's skill aren't Devices: make them as ordinary items and improve them with upgrades. What powers a Device depends on the setting: clockwork, alchemy, runes, lightning, or something stranger. Each Device has a rating from 1 to 5 and a single function.

  • Rolling: when a Device makes a roll, it rolls twice its rating in dice, like an NPC rolling something it's proficient in (see NPCs). Setting it or starting it takes 1 action unless it says otherwise.
  • Charges: a Device has charges equal to its rating. Each use spends 1. Each Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge, like a kit.
  • Who can build one: Devices are complex work. You need Crafts 3+ and Technology 2+ to build one, and its rating can't be higher than your Crafts dots − 2 (rating 1 at Crafts 3, 2 at Crafts 4, 3 at Crafts 5).
  • Crafting: a rating 1–2 Device is Complex (target 10); rating 3 or more is Masterwork (target 15). Each Device also needs a core component the setting defines, such as a power source or a focus. Without one, the Device can't be built.
  • Disabling or bypassing: roll Dexterity or Intelligence + Technology, contested by the Device's roll. On a success, it's disarmed, jammed or slipped past; on a failure, it triggers or alerts its owner, as fits the Device.
  • Settings add their own Devices, and decide what core components are and where to find them. Higher-rated Devices can also be found, bought or salvaged.
In Duskworld

Glints

Glints are the core component of every Duskworld Device. Each glint has a rating from 1 to 5, which is its number of charges. The Device's own rating still sets its dice and the crafting limit, and any glint fits any Device.

GlintRatingWhere it comes fromRefill
Forged glint1–3Mendris's workshops, copying kren techEach Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge
Kren glint4–5Storage compartments in the abandoned kren factories of the DusklandsRefills to full at the end of each rest
  • Charges: a Device spends 1 charge from its glint each time it's used. A Complication on a Device roll spends 1 more.
  • Running dry: a glint that reaches 0 charges is empty for good, and the Device stops working until it's refitted. Neither rest, downtime nor Field Repair revives an empty glint. A rating 1 glint is single-use.
  • Downtime: the end of a week of downtime refills every glint that isn't empty.
  • Burning out: when a Device spends its glint's last charge, you can burn it out on purpose: the Device rolls with +2d and raises the stakes.
  • Refitting: putting a new glint into a Device takes an hour of work and needs Technology 2+.
  • Empty glints: an empty forged glint is scrap. An empty kren glint becomes a shell, Mendris's largest unit of money.

Buying and trading

Forged glints are legal, and are bought and sold like other gear.

Forged glintPrice
Rating 12 handfuls of boxings
Rating 21 slab
Rating 33 slabs

Kren glints are another matter. Selling one is illegal: they belong to the Houses.

  • Surrendering a kren glint to a House earns favors equal to its rating with that House (see Favors).
  • Fencing one gets its rating in slabs, a fraction of what it's worth. Finding a buyer is a Manipulation + Streetwise roll; on a Complication, someone notices.
