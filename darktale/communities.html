The communities

Communities are defined by each setting. Select a setting to see its communities; each has a page with its traits.

Duskworld

CommunityFrontier FolkThe people of the fortified farmsteads (or homesteads) around Dawnkeep: working the fields, standing watch, and living close to the edge of the Dusklands. The farms on Northbank's northern edge are the closest thing the city has to them.CommunityHousebornThe royals of Mendris: members of one of the five Great Houses, or of the many Vassal Houses sworn to them. Most Houseborn belong to a Vassal House; a Great House name opens more doors, and draws more eyes. Their estates and townhouses fill Dawnkeep's prominent Sectors: the Midlands, seat of the Great Council, the Caliethan University and the trade of the elite, and Northbank, with its fine homes and stores.CommunityClerkfolkThe families who keep Mendris's paperwork moving: the clerks, registrars, bookkeepers and petty officials of the Midlands' bureaus and the Great Council's offices, and the merchants, factors and shareholders of the merchant district and Northbank's counting-houses. Community traits

A community is a group of people you belong to: where you live, who looks out for you, and what that life teaches. At character creation, you choose the community you grew up in.

Each community is a small tree of community traits, read like a vocation's talent tree (see Talents):

  • Key trait: every member of that community gains it.
  • Further traits: one or two more, each with an after tag and other prerequisites (skills, attributes, race).
  • At character creation, you gain your community's key trait. Its further traits come later through advancement, or you can take one with your free pick at character creation.
  • Community skills: each community lists two or three skills. Put 1 skill dot in one of them.

Key traits are about as strong as a secondary racial trait, and lean toward social and story hooks. A further trait costs the same as a talent, at creation and in advancement.

Joining another community

Characters can join other communities during play, the way they can train into another vocation.

  • Gain its key trait through advancement, at the cost of a talent. After that, its further traits are open to you like your own community's.
  • No skill dot. The community skill dot is only for the community you grew up in.
  • You keep your first community. Joining a new one adds to who you are; it doesn't replace it.

The cost is the only requirement, but joining a community should mean something in the story. GMs are encouraged to work with the player on an in-world reason and a chance to roleplay it: a season travelling with a caravan, a debt repaid to a guild, a winter sheltered on a Frontier farmstead.

A character can also start play as a member of a second community, using the free pick at character creation. Their backstory covers how they came to belong to it.
