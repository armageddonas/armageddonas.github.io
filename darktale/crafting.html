Crafting

How characters make, improve and repair things, and the Devices they can build. Crafting is an extended action: everything on that page applies unless this one says otherwise.

Making an item

Choose what you're making, gather the materials, and roll at the interval below until you reach the target.

  • Pool: Intelligence + Crafts. Use Technology for the setting's most advanced machines, and Science for chemicals and compounds. A tool kit gives +1d as usual (see Kits).
  • Target: set by the item's complexity, plus any upgrades you declare (below).
  • Interval: an hour for small items, a day for weapons and armor, a week for large or heavy work.
  • Attempts: you can make a number of rolls up to your dice pool when you start (Intelligence + Crafts, plus a kit or specialty). Unlike other extended actions, crafting has this limit as well as the Stress cost below. If you run out before reaching the target, see Defects.
  • Materials: half the item's price in wealth (see Wealth), or salvaged and found materials at the GM's call.
ComplexityTargetExamples
Simple3Knife, club, rope ladder, a simple trap
Standard6Sword, spear, light armor, a kit
Complex10Crossbow, medium armor, a lock, a rating 1–2 Device
Masterwork15Heavy armor, a rifle, a rating 3+ Device

As in any extended action, a roll with no successes costs 1 Stress to press on; if you don't pay, you stop there, as if you'd run out of attempts. A Complication costs a success, time or materials. An Opportunity can halve the interval, save materials, or turn up something useful; it never adds successes.

Defects

If you run out of attempts with at least half the target (counting declared upgrades), the item is finished but defective: it gets no upgrades and one defect from the table below. With less than half, the work fails and the materials are lost.

A defect can be fixed later as a repair, like broken gear: the target is half the item's crafting target, rounded up.

ItemDefect
WeaponUnreliable: a Complication on an attack with it breaks it
ArmorIll-fitting: its Stealth and Athletics penalty is 1 die worse (light armor gains −1d)
Kit2 charges instead of 3
Device1 charge fewer, and a Complication when using it spends all its charges
OtherCrude: it works, but anyone who knows the craft can tell (−1d to Socialize where it's seen)

Upgrades

An upgrade improves an item beyond the ordinary. An item can hold up to your Crafts dots in upgrades, counting an advanced upgrade as two.

Declared upgrades

Before you start, declare the upgrades you want. Each one adds 2 to the target, and each advanced upgrade adds 4. That's cheaper than refining, but you risk more: if you run out of attempts before reaching the full target, you lose every declared upgrade and the item has a defect or fails (see Defects).

Refining

Once you reach the target, you can push for more with the attempts you have left:

  • Every 3 successes from these rolls add one upgrade (an advanced upgrade needs 6). That costs more than declaring it, but the item itself is already finished and safe.
  • A roll with no successes, or a Complication, ends refining, and the Complication costs you one upgrade gained by refining.
  • You can stop after any roll and keep what you've gained.

Upgrade list

The GM can approve others that fit the item.

Repair

Repairing uses the same rules, including the attempt limit, at the interval for the item.

  • Lost armor rating: target 3 for each point of rating restored.
  • Broken gear: half the item's crafting target, rounded up.
  • Materials: a quarter of the item's price, or salvage.

Repair is how armor regains lost rating and how broken gear works again (see Armor and Rest).

Devices

A Device is a self-operating mechanism: a trap, an alarm, a launcher, an automaton. Once it's set or started, it works on its own, so the user's skill doesn't matter. Weapons, armor and tools whose use depends on the wielder's skill aren't Devices: make them as ordinary items and improve them with upgrades. What powers a Device depends on the setting: clockwork, alchemy, runes, lightning, or something stranger. Each Device has a rating from 1 to 5 and a single function.

  • Rolling: when a Device makes a roll, it rolls twice its rating in dice, like an NPC rolling something it's proficient in (see NPCs). Setting it or starting it takes 1 action unless it says otherwise.
  • Charges: a Device has charges equal to its rating. Each use spends 1. Each Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge, like a kit.
  • Who can build one: Devices are complex work. You need Crafts 3+ and Technology 2+ to build one, and its rating can't be higher than your Crafts dots − 2 (rating 1 at Crafts 3, 2 at Crafts 4, 3 at Crafts 5).
  • Crafting: a rating 1–2 Device is Complex (target 10); rating 3 or more is Masterwork (target 15). Each Device also needs a core component the setting defines, such as a power source or a focus. Without one, the Device can't be built.
  • Disabling or bypassing: roll Dexterity or Intelligence + Technology, contested by the Device's roll. On a success, it's disarmed, jammed or slipped past; on a failure, it triggers or alerts its owner, as fits the Device.
  • Settings add their own Devices, and decide what core components are and where to find them. Higher-rated Devices can also be found, bought or salvaged.
