Crafting

Crafting

How characters make, improve and repair things, and the Devices they can build. Crafting is an extended action: everything on that page applies unless this one says otherwise.

Making an item

Choose what you're making, gather the materials, and roll at the interval below until you reach the target.

  • Pool: Intelligence + Crafts. Use Technology for the setting's most advanced machines, and Science for chemicals and compounds. A tool kit gives +1d as usual (see Kits).
  • Target: set by the item's complexity, plus any upgrades you declare (below).
  • Interval: set by the item's size, its bulk: an hour for items with no bulk, a day for bulk 1–2, a week for bulk 3 or more. A Device uses the bulk of what it's built into, or a day.
  • Attempts: you can make a number of rolls up to your base pool: Intelligence + Crafts, plus a specialty that applies. Temporary bonuses don't count (a kit's +1d, pushing, teamwork, conditions or magic), though they still add dice to each roll as usual. Unlike other extended actions, crafting has this limit as well as the Stress cost below. If you run out before reaching the target, see Defects.
  • Materials: half the item's price, rounded up, at least 1 handful (see Wealth), or salvaged and found materials at the GM's call. Prices are in the weapon, armor, kit and equipment tables; Devices are priced below.

A Device's target comes from its rating, 6 plus 3 for each rating above 1 (see Devices).

As in any extended action, a roll with no successes costs 1 Stress to press on; if you don't pay, you stop there, as if you'd run out of attempts. A Complication costs a success, time or materials. An Opportunity can halve the interval, save materials, or turn up something useful; it never adds successes.

Defects

If you run out of attempts with at least half the target (counting declared upgrades), the item is finished but defective: it gets no upgrades and one defect from the table below. With less than half, the work fails and the materials are lost.

A defect can be fixed later as a repair, like broken gear: the target is half the item's crafting target, rounded up.

ItemDefect
WeaponUnreliable: a Complication on an attack with it breaks it
ArmorIll-fitting: its Stealth and Athletics penalty is 1 die worse (light armor gains −1d)
Kit2 charges instead of 3
Device1 charge fewer, and the GM can spend a Complication when using it to drain all its charges
OtherCrude: it works, but anyone who knows the craft can tell (−1d to Socialize where it's seen)

Upgrades

An upgrade improves an item beyond the ordinary. An item can hold up to your Crafts dots in upgrades, counting an advanced upgrade as two.

Declared upgrades

Before you start, declare the upgrades you want. Each one adds 2 to the target, and each advanced upgrade adds 4. That's cheaper than refining, but you risk more: if you run out of attempts before reaching the full target, you lose every declared upgrade and the item has a defect or fails (see Defects).

Refining

Once you reach the target, you can push for more with the attempts you have left:

  • Every 3 successes from these rolls add one upgrade (an advanced upgrade needs 6). That costs more than declaring it, but the item itself is already finished and safe.
  • A roll with no successes, or a Complication, ends refining, and the Complication costs you one upgrade gained by refining.
  • You can stop after any roll and keep what you've gained.

Upgrade list

The GM can approve others that fit the item.

Repair

Repairing uses the same rules, including the attempt limit, at the interval for the item.

  • Lost armor rating: target 3 for each point of rating restored.
  • Broken gear: half the item's crafting target, rounded up.
  • Materials: a quarter of the item's price, or salvage.

Repair is how armor regains lost rating and how broken gear works again (see Armor and Rest).

Devices

A Device is a complex machine: a trap, an alarm, a launcher, an instrument, an automaton. It needs a core component and the skill to build it (below), and has a rating from 1 to 5 and a single function. Otherwise it's used like any item: you pick it up, aim it, set it or switch it on, and roll your own pool when its function calls for a roll. Ordinary weapons, armor and tools aren't Devices: make them as ordinary items and improve them with upgrades. What powers a Device depends on the setting: clockwork, alchemy, runes, lightning, or something stranger.

  • Using one: setting, starting or using a Device takes 1 action unless it says otherwise. When its function calls for a roll, you roll the pool it names.
  • Automated Devices work on their own once they're set or started, and are marked Automated. When one makes a roll, it rolls twice its rating in dice, like an NPC rolling something it's proficient in (see NPCs), and your skill doesn't matter.
  • Rating sets a Device's charges, its scale (range, area, duration, people affected, as its function says), how hard it is to disable, and, for an Automated Device, its dice.
  • Charges: a Device has charges equal to its rating. Each use spends 1. Each Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge, like a kit. At 0 charges it stops working until it's refilled; only a Device that says it's single-use is spent for good.
  • Burning the core: before a roll a Device makes or powers, you can burn out its core component: the roll gets +2d and raises the stakes, and the core is destroyed. The Device doesn't work again until a new core is fitted.
  • Who can build one: Devices are complex work. You need Crafts 3+ and Technology 2+ to build one, and its rating can't be higher than your Crafts dots − 2 (rating 1 at Crafts 3, 2 at Crafts 4, 3 at Crafts 5).
  • Crafting: choose the rating before you start. The target is 6 for rating 1, +3 for each rating above it: 9 at rating 2, 12 at 3, 15 at 4, 18 at 5. Each Device also needs a core component the setting defines, such as a power source or a focus. Without one, the Device can't be built.
  • Disabling or bypassing: roll Dexterity or Intelligence + Technology, contested by twice the Device's rating, whether it's Automated or not. On a success, it's disarmed, jammed or slipped past; on a failure, it triggers or alerts its owner, as fits the Device.
  • Price: by rating, not counting the core component (table below). Materials for crafting one are half that, as for any item.
  • Settings add their own Devices, and decide what core components are and where to find them. Higher-rated Devices can also be found, bought or salvaged.

Glints

Glints are the core component of every Duskworld Device. Each glint has a rating from 1 to 5, which is its number of charges. The Device's own rating still sets its dice and the crafting limit, and any glint fits any Device.

GlintRatingWhere it comes fromRefill
Forged glint1–3Mendris's workshops, copying kren techEach Prepare at a rest refills 1 charge
Kren glint4–5Storage compartments in the abandoned kren factories of the DusklandsRefills to full at the end of each rest
  • Charges: a Device spends 1 charge from its glint each time it's used. The GM can spend a Complication on a Device roll to drain 1 more.
  • Running dry: a glint at 0 charges stops powering its Device until it's refilled. Only a glint that says it's single-use, like the flashstick's, is spent for good.
  • Downtime: the end of a week of downtime refills every glint that isn't burnt out.
  • Burning out: a glint is the core you burn when burning the core: before a roll its Device or runetech item powers, you can burn the glint out for +2d, raising the stakes. The glint cracks, and it's scrap, forged or kren.
  • Refitting: putting a new glint into a Device takes an hour of work and needs Technology 2+.
  • Shells: only the Houses can drain a kren glint without cracking it. The empty, whole glint is a shell, Mendris's largest unit of money, and only House Merovant cuts shells into slabs and boxings.

Buying and trading

Forged glints are legal, and are bought and sold like other gear.

Forged glintPrice
Rating 12 handfuls of boxings
Rating 21 slab
Rating 33 slabs

Kren glints are another matter. Selling one is illegal: they belong to the Houses.

  • Surrendering a kren glint to a House earns favors equal to its rating with that House (see Favors).
  • Fencing one gets its rating in slabs, a fraction of what it's worth. Finding a buyer is a Manipulation + Streetwise roll; on a Complication, someone notices.
